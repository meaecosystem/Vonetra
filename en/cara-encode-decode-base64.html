How to Encode and Decode Text to Base64

Found a weird string made up of only letters, numbers, and an "=" sign at the end? That's probably base64. Here's how to turn text into base64 and back.

October 1, 2026 · 2 min read

Base64 is a way of representing binary data (or any text) using a combination of letters, numbers, and a few symbols, so it can be safely transmitted through systems that only support plain text — for example, embedded in a URL, JSON, or email. Important note: base64 is encoding, not encryption — anyone can decode it back, so don't use it to hide sensitive data.

Base64 isn't encryption — here's the difference

Encoding and encryption are often confused, but they're very different. Encryption needs a key to unlock the data again, while base64 needs no key at all — anyone who knows it's base64 can decode it instantly and read the original content. So if your goal is to hide sensitive information like a password, base64 isn't the answer.

Why does Unicode support matter?

Base64 was originally designed for standard ASCII characters. If the text you're encoding contains emoji or non-Latin characters (Arabic, Japanese, Chinese scripts, and the like), an encoder that doesn't handle Unicode properly can produce corrupted results when decoded back. This tool handles Unicode correctly, so any text — emoji included — can be safely encoded and decoded without losing characters.

Frequently asked questions

Is my text sent to a server?

No. The encode and decode process runs directly in your browser.

Is base64 safe for storing passwords?

No. Base64 can be decoded by anyone with no key required, so it shouldn't be used to hide sensitive data.

Why does my decoded result come out garbled or error out?

The text you pasted probably isn't valid base64 — a character may have been dropped or altered when it was copied.
