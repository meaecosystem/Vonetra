How to Resize an Image Without Distorting It

Need a profile photo with a specific pixel size, or want to shrink an image so it's not too large? Here's how to set its dimensions, with an aspect-ratio lock so it doesn't stretch or squash.

Image Resizer changes an image's dimensions (width and height in pixels), with the option to lock the aspect ratio so the proportions stay intact, or leave it free when you genuinely want to change the shape.

Steps to resize an image

  1. Open tools.meaecosystem.com/image-resizer.
  2. Upload the image you want to resize.
  3. Enter the new width and height in pixels, or use the "Quick size" option to resize by a percentage of the original (say, 50%).
  4. Make sure the aspect-ratio lock is on if you want the proportions to stay intact — changing one number automatically adjusts the other.
  5. If the output will be JPEG or WebP, also set the quality level.
  6. Download the resulting image.

Why did my image come out stretched or squashed?

This happens when the aspect-ratio lock is off and you change the width or height separately without accounting for the original proportions. If your goal is just to shrink or enlarge the image without changing its shape, keep the aspect-ratio lock on.

When should you deliberately turn off the aspect-ratio lock?

Sometimes you genuinely need fixed dimensions that aren't proportional to the original image — say, a platform requires a banner size with a specific ratio. In that case, turn off the aspect-ratio lock and enter the required dimensions manually, even if the result ends up looking slightly different in proportion from the original.

Frequently asked questions

Is my image uploaded to a server?

No. The resizing process runs directly in your browser.

Does enlarging an image reduce its quality?

Yes, usually. Enlarging an image beyond its original size forces the software to "guess" additional pixels, which often makes the result look blurrier or less sharp than the original.

Which formats are supported?

PNG, JPG, WebP, and any other image format your browser supports.
