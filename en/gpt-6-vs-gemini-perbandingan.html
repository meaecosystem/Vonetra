GPT-6 vs Gemini: Which Is Better? An Honest 2026 Comparison

OpenAI and Google both have their newest flagship models out this year — GPT-6 Astra and Gemini 3.1 Pro. Before diving into the numbers, there's one important thing rarely mentioned in other comparison articles: the two weren't released at the same time. That makes the comparison a bit more complicated than just "which one wins."

October 2, 2026 · 5 min read

An important note before we start: this isn't a fair fight timing-wise

Google released Gemini 3.1 Pro on February 19, 2026. OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra on September 3, 2026 — seven months later. In the fast-moving world of AI, a gap that size is significant. So if GPT-6 Astra leads on many benchmarks, that's partly expected simply because it's the newer model. A fairer comparison would actually be to wait for Google's next-generation Gemini, but since that doesn't exist yet as of this writing, the comparison below is still based on the latest model currently available from each lab.

On raw capability (benchmarks)

Several independent benchmarking organizations report GPT-6 Astra leading on most of the benchmarks tested between the two — including complex reasoning, long multi-step tasks, and coding. It's worth noting: the exact figures reported vary by organization, since each has its own testing methodology — so treat these numbers as a general directional picture, not absolute figures that hold across every use case.

Source: Artificial Analysis, as of September 2026. Scores in percent except Intelligence Index.
Benchmark GPT-6 Astra Gemini 3.1 Pro
Intelligence Index 53 30
Humanity's Last Exam 55% 47%
Terminal-Bench 4.0 59% 4%
SciCode 56% 59%
AA-LCR (long context) 81% 82%

Of these five benchmarks, GPT-6 Astra leads on three, and Gemini 3.1 Pro leads narrowly on two (SciCode and long-context comprehension). So it isn't a clean sweep across the board — on certain tasks like science coding and reading long documents, the two are nearly even.

On pricing, the gap is large

This is where Gemini 3.1 Pro wins decisively. For API usage, Gemini is far cheaper in nearly every scenario — especially at high usage volume.

The gap runs about four to five times across nearly every pricing component. For developers or businesses using the API at high volume — say, summarizing long documents or running repeated agent loops — this gap accumulates into a real difference in operating cost, not a small number you can ignore.

Note: the prices above are based on standard listings as of September 2026 and can change at any time — always check OpenAI's and Google's official pages for the latest figures before making a decision.

On context window (how much it can "remember" in one conversation)

Both have a context window of around 1 million tokens via the API — so they're nearly equal on paper. The difference shows up on the consumer-app side: in the ChatGPT app, the context window is tiered by plan (smaller on the free tier, larger on paid plans), while Gemini provides the full 1 million tokens starting from the Google AI Pro plan and up, with its free tier capped at around 32,000 tokens.

On features ordinary consumers feel day to day

Beyond benchmarks and API pricing, here's what's more relevant for most people who use these through the app rather than the API:

  • Multimodal: Gemini has an edge in native audio and video processing within a single conversation. ChatGPT focuses more on text, images, and files.
  • Ecosystem integration: Gemini wins if you already live inside the Google ecosystem (Gmail, Docs, Drive, Maps). ChatGPT has a broader plugin and third-party tools ecosystem.
  • Free tier: Several comparisons note Gemini's free tier is more "generous" — larger message and image quotas, plus access to several Gemini 3 models at once (though with a capped context window). ChatGPT's free tier has also shown ads in some regions since early 2026.
  • Agentic/coding capability: GPT-6 is designed to be strong at agentic coding and computer use (AI that can operate desktop apps). Gemini has the Antigravity IDE and strength in code generation for large codebases.

So, which one is better?

The honest answer: it depends on your needs, and on when you're reading this article. If what you're after is the highest raw capability available right now and cost isn't a concern, GPT-6 Astra currently leads on most public benchmarks. If what you're after is cost efficiency at high usage volume, or you need native audio/video processing, Gemini 3.1 Pro is clearly the more sensible choice. For casual everyday use through the free apps, both are more than adequate, with Gemini slightly more generous on free quota.

What's certain is that, because of this seven-month release gap, this comparison is likely to shift once Google releases its next-generation Gemini. AI model competition moves fast — the figures above are a snapshot of conditions as of early October 2026, not a permanent conclusion.

Frequently asked questions

Is this comparison fair given the release-date gap?

Not entirely. Gemini 3.1 Pro released in February 2026, GPT-6 Astra released in September 2026. GPT-6 Astra's lead on many benchmarks partly reflects that it's simply a model seven months newer, not solely that OpenAI is consistently "better."

Which is cheaper for everyday use?

For app-based usage (the ChatGPT or Gemini app), both main paid plans are priced similarly, around $20/month. The significant price difference is actually on the developer API side, where Gemini 3.1 Pro is far cheaper per token.

Are the benchmark figures in this article definitely accurate?

The benchmark figures come from several independent benchmarking organizations, each with its own methodology, and some of them don't always agree exactly on the numbers. Use them as a general directional picture, and if you need precise figures for an important decision, it's best to check the official benchmark pages from OpenAI and Google directly.
